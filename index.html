ADAM

I joined the creation of a new association. What began as a website became an exercise in designing the way the association communicates, receives members and works behind the scenes.

MY POSITION / O MEU PAPEL

Secretary of the Board · website administration · communication & marketing · UX and member-system specification

THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE INTERFACE / 2026
01

THE STARTING POINT

First, we needed to exist publicly.

ADAM was new. Before thinking about member dashboards or rewards, I had to think about the questions somebody encountering us for the first time would have: Who are you? What do you do? Why join? Where can I play? What is happening next?

DECISIONThe site should not behave like a static institutional page. I structured it to be useful to the wider airsoft community too, with events and directories for teams and fields alongside the association itself.

COMMUNITY ↗EVENTS ↗JOIN ↗
02

THE TURNING POINT

“Join us” was not the end of the journey.

Once registration became real, a new set of questions appeared. What happens after the form? How do we review someone? How does the person know they are active? What happens when the quota expires? How do they renew without becoming a duplicate member?

SHIFTAt this point I stopped thinking in pages and started thinking in states, transitions and responsibilities.

PENDING→ACTIVE→RENEWAL PENDING→EXPIRED
03

ITERATING THE MEMBER AREA

What does a member actually need to see first?

The member area went through repeated layout decisions. I kept returning to the same hierarchy problem: identity and status had to be immediate, the digital card needed more prominence, and account actions could not compete with the information members came there to check.

EARLY data + notifications + status + cardCHANGE bring the card forwardCURRENT identity → status → card → actions

MAKING MEMBERSHIP TANGIBLE

The card kept revealing new design problems.

A digital card sounds simple until it has to work. The QR needed to remain clear enough to validate, the PDF could not crop information, print needed safe margins, mobile could not simply shrink the desktop card, and expired members needed a different experience without losing their account.

ITERATIONEach technical constraint changed the interface. I treated those failures as design feedback rather than edge cases.

05

DESIGNING FROM BOTH SIDES

I also have to use the system behind the scenes.

Being Secretary of the Board changes the way I approach the interface. I am not designing an abstract admin panel for somebody else: applicant review, member searches, status changes, quota information and history are processes I need the association to handle reliably.

PRINCIPLEA member-facing improvement is only successful if the administrative side can support it without creating more manual work.

APPLICATIONAPPROVALMEMBER HISTORYQUOTARENEWALVALIDATION
06

GROWING WITH REAL USE

New ideas came from using the association, not from a feature checklist.

Events led to QR check-in. Participation led to points. Points led to cosmetic rewards and titles. Communication needs led to clearer notification and WhatsApp structures. The system grew because real activity exposed the next useful problem to solve.

EVENT→CHECK-IN→POINTS→REWARDS

COMMUNICATION / PHOTOGRAPHY

The digital system still has to feel connected to the people using it.

Alongside the platform, I work on the association's communication, website content and photography. These are not separate projects to me; they are different points of contact with the same community.

WHAT I TAKE FROM IT

ADAM taught me to design less like I was finishing a screen and more like I was maintaining a living system.

The most useful decisions have rarely come from making something more decorative. They have come from noticing friction while the association is actually being used, understanding who that friction affects, and changing the system without breaking the rest of the journey. The project is still evolving — which is precisely what makes it valuable to my practice.

Painting
2019—2020

Small paintings based on stills selected from South Korean music videos. Each image began with a moment chosen for the emotion it conveyed.

By removing those moments from movement, sound and narrative and translating them into paint, I explored how that emotion could remain—or change—inside a static image. The paintings were made independently; visual sets were organised only afterwards.
